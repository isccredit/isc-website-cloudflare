Why it is worth reading

Some of these orders had a less expensive route.

When a missing bureau is caused by a security freeze, a bureau copy may resolve it once the borrower lifts the freeze, without a second full credit report, and only the missing bureaus are charged. Both routes are legitimate orders. One costs less, and until the two sit side by side on a page there is no way to know which one your team reached for.

The same goes for a borrower pulled twice in the same week, or a paid pull that came back without a usable score and was never followed up. Waypoint puts them in front of you, named file by file. It is not the kind of thing a vendor is obliged to point out. We would rather you had it.

What a Waypoint review covers

Operational figures come from your company's own ISC ordering and billing activity. Any modeled information is separately identified and attributed.

Monthly

The pipeline

Every file still waiting on a bureau at month end, oldest first, with days open and the bureau named. Files cleared this month, and files from earlier months still moving, such as a supplement or a DU retrieval.

Monthly

Loan officer review

One row per loan officer: orders, spend, median score against the company, files still waiting, repeat pulls and the services each one uses. Sorted by volume, never ranked.

Monthly

Repeat pulls, with a reason

Every borrower pulled more than once, each with what the file shows. A re-pull after a rapid rescore is explained. A missing bureau answered with a full pull instead of a bureau copy is worth a question.

Monthly and quarterly

Branch by branch

For companies with more than one office, a company summary comes first, then each branch on its own pages in its own color. A branch manager can be handed just their branch.

Monthly

Billing detail, for the accountant

Every dollar ISC billed, tied back to a file, and what was paid by card or SmartPay at the time of order versus billed to the company. It closes the company summary. Your ISC invoice remains the statement of record.

Quarterly

The direction over three months

How volume, spend, the credit profile of the files being ordered and product use moved across three months, branch by branch. A steady median can sit on top of a mix that is moving, and that is where it shows.

METHODOLOGY

Sources. This account's own credit order and billing records held by ISC Credit for August 1 - 31, 2026.

Counting files. 75 credit files were opened in the period. 2 are frozen bureau copies of another file, 17 are split or edited copies of an existing order (a joint report split in two, a spouse removed, or a bureau added) and 0 are refresh orders. Borrower order counts are 56, counting each original order once.

Matching. Files are grouped by applicant name. Reference numbers are reused across borrowers and months, so they are shown but not used to group.

Middle score. The middle of the three bureau scores, used only where all three returned a score. ISC does not adjust, estimate or fill in a missing score.

Frozen bureau copy. Charged per bureau recovered, counted across both the applicant and the co-applicant. For this account the rate is $16 per bureau.

Who paid. Card payments taken at the time of order, by the ordering user or by the borrower through SmartPay, are matched to the file they paid for. What is left is billed to the company. The ISC invoice is the statement of record for what is due.

Branch. The branch set for the ordering user on the ISC account. Users without one are shown as "No branch on file".

Repeat pulls. A second or later paid bureau product for the same borrower within the period. Each is marked explained when the file shows a reason, such as a rapid rescore or a soft pull followed by the hard pull, and worth a question when it does not. A missing bureau answered with a full pull rather than a bureau copy is worth a question.

Days open. Counted from the order date to the last day of August.

Worth a second look. Repeat pulls worth a question plus paid pulls that returned no middle score. A file is counted once even when it appears in both.

Cost per order. Everything billed to a user, divided by that user's borrower orders. It includes supplements, copies and verifications, so it measures total spend per file rather than the price of a credit report.

Figures shown by user. Sorted by volume, not ranked. They count files and orders, and are not a measure of effort, skill or results.

What this does not do. It does not assess borrower eligibility, predict an approval outcome, rate individual employees, or promise that any score can be improved.

METHODOLOGY

Sources. This account's own credit order and billing records held by ISC Credit for August 1 - 31, 2026.

Counting files. 75 credit files were opened in the period. 2 are frozen bureau copies of another file, 17 are split or edited copies of an existing order (a joint report split in two, a spouse removed, or a bureau added) and 0 are refresh orders. Borrower order counts are 56, counting each original order once.

Matching. Files are grouped by applicant name. Reference numbers are reused across borrowers and months, so they are shown but not used to group.

Middle score. The middle of the three bureau scores, used only where all three returned a score. ISC does not adjust, estimate or fill in a missing score.

Frozen bureau copy. Charged per bureau recovered, counted across both the applicant and the co-applicant. For this account the rate is $16 per bureau.

Who paid. Card payments taken at the time of order, by the ordering user or by the borrower through SmartPay, are matched to the file they paid for. What is left is billed to the company. The ISC invoice is the statement of record for what is due.

Branch. The branch set for the ordering user on the ISC account. Users without one are shown as "No branch on file".

Repeat pulls. A second or later paid bureau product for the same borrower within the period. Each is marked explained when the file shows a reason, such as a rapid rescore or a soft pull followed by the hard pull, and worth a question when it does not. A missing bureau answered with a full pull rather than a bureau copy is worth a question.

Days open. Counted from the order date to the last day of August.

Worth a second look. Repeat pulls worth a question plus paid pulls that returned no middle score. A file is counted once even when it appears in both.

Cost per order. Everything billed to a user, divided by that user's borrower orders. It includes supplements, copies and verifications, so it measures total spend per file rather than the price of a credit report.

Figures shown by user. Sorted by volume, not ranked. They count files and orders, and are not a measure of effort, skill or results.

What this does not do. It does not assess borrower eligibility, predict an approval outcome, rate individual employees, or promise that any score can be improved.
