Why it is worth reading

Some of these orders had a less expensive route.

When a missing bureau is caused by a security freeze, a bureau copy may resolve it once the borrower lifts the freeze, without a second full credit report, and only the missing bureaus are charged. Both routes are legitimate orders. One costs less, and until the two sit side by side on a page there is no way to know which one your team reached for.

The same goes for a borrower pulled twice in the same week, or a paid pull that came back without a usable score and was never followed up. Waypoint puts them in front of you, named file by file. It is not the kind of thing a vendor is obliged to point out. We would rather you had it.

METHODOLOGY

Sources. This account's own credit order and billing records held by ISC Credit for September 1 - 30, 2026.

Counting files. 122 credit files were opened in the period. 8 are frozen bureau copies of another file and 2 are refresh orders. Borrower order counts are 112, counting each original order once.

Matching. Files are grouped by applicant name. Reference numbers are reused across borrowers and months, so they are shown but not used to group.

Middle score. The middle of the three bureau scores, used only where all three returned a score. ISC does not adjust, estimate or fill in a missing score.

Frozen bureau copy. Charged per bureau recovered, counted across both the applicant and the co-applicant. For this account the rate is $15 per bureau.

Repeat pulls. A second or later paid bureau product for the same borrower within the period. One file counts in two sections, so those file counts are not additive.

Cost per order. Everything billed to a user, divided by that user's borrower orders. It includes supplements, copies and verifications, so it measures total spend per file rather than the price of a credit report. Left blank below three orders.

Figures shown by user. Sorted by volume, not ranked. They count files and orders, and are not a measure of effort, skill or results.

What this does not do. It does not assess borrower eligibility, predict an approval outcome, rate individual employees, or promise that any score can be improved.

METHODOLOGY

Sources. This account's own credit order and billing records held by ISC Credit for September 1 - 30, 2026.

Counting files. 122 credit files were opened in the period. 8 are frozen bureau copies of another file and 2 are refresh orders. Borrower order counts are 112, counting each original order once.

Matching. Files are grouped by applicant name. Reference numbers are reused across borrowers and months, so they are shown but not used to group.

Middle score. The middle of the three bureau scores, used only where all three returned a score. ISC does not adjust, estimate or fill in a missing score.

Frozen bureau copy. Charged per bureau recovered, counted across both the applicant and the co-applicant. For this account the rate is $15 per bureau.

Repeat pulls. A second or later paid bureau product for the same borrower within the period. One file counts in two sections, so those file counts are not additive.

Cost per order. Everything billed to a user, divided by that user's borrower orders. It includes supplements, copies and verifications, so it measures total spend per file rather than the price of a credit report. Left blank below three orders.

Figures shown by user. Sorted by volume, not ranked. They count files and orders, and are not a measure of effort, skill or results.

What this does not do. It does not assess borrower eligibility, predict an approval outcome, rate individual employees, or promise that any score can be improved.
